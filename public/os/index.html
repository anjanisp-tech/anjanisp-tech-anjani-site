Where most founders are

Four levels of using AI in a business.

Most founders stop at level 2. The real gains start at level 4, when AI stops being something you open and becomes something that runs.

Level 1

Ask and copy

You ask a question, copy the answer, and start fresh every time. It knows nothing about your business.

Level 2

Better prompts

Longer chats and saved instructions. The answers improve, but you still drive every step by hand.

Level 3

A pile of tools

Automations, plug-ins and scripts. Useful, but scattered. When one breaks, nobody notices for weeks.

Level 4 · this page

One running system

It remembers your business, follows your rules, works on a schedule and tells you when something breaks.

The one rule that keeps it running

Use the simplest tool that does the job.

Before anything new is added, the system checks whether something already in place can do it. Built-in first. Official apps next. Custom code last.

Most AI setups fail the same way: too many tools, each added on a good day, none of them owned. Every extra tool is one more thing that can break while you are busy. This rule keeps the stack small, and a small stack is one you can trust.

  1. 01What the computer already doesFiles, folders, schedules. Free and the most stable.
  2. 02The vendor's own command toolsBuilt and maintained by the company that made the service.
  3. 03Features built into ClaudeScheduled jobs, browser help, built-in skills. Nothing extra to install.
  4. 04Official app connectionsNotion, Gmail, Calendar and the like, linked the supported way.
  5. 05Add-on packsHandy for one kind of work, but heavier to run.
  6. 06Custom scripts, then manual workLast resort. Every custom script is a future repair job.

Learned the hard way

What the system taught me.

One lesson from each hard week, newest first. The full stories are in Chapter 06.

  1. Week of 2026-08-17

    "Not found" is not "not there".

    In one day, the system reported three times that some work had never been done. All three were wrong. Twice it had looked in the wrong place, and once it had searched with the wrong words. Now a "missing" finding must search more than one way before anyone believes it.

  2. Week of 2026-08-17

    A job that waits for permission at 3 a.m. simply stops.

    Every scheduled job was still set to ask before acting. With nobody awake to say yes, they quietly gave up halfway. The fix was to decide in advance what each job may do on its own, and to keep the risky actions behind a yes.

  3. Week of 2026-07-13

    A number you cannot measure yet is a blocker, not a zero.

    Some targets showed as failures only because the data had not built up yet. Marking them "still collecting, ready by this date" stopped the false alarms, and the real gaps stood out.

  4. Week of 2026-07-06

    Check twice before you call something broken.

    Files looked damaged while they were still syncing. The fix was to read again after a pause, not to "repair" files that were never broken.

  5. Week of 2026-06-29

    Privacy checks must fail closed.

    A check that removed private details from text could not see inside tables. Now anything it cannot confirm as clean does not get published.

Questions founders ask

Before you start.

Do I need to be technical?

For the Sprint, no. I build it with you and you use it in plain language. For the free kit, you or someone on your team should be comfortable editing a few text files.

Which plan do I need?

A paid Claude plan with projects and memory. The ideas work with any AI, but the kit's examples assume Claude.

How long until it helps?

The kit takes about 30 minutes to set up. Expect one to two weeks of real use before it starts paying back. The Sprint ends with something already doing real work.

What is the difference between the kit and the Sprint?

The kit is an empty frame. The Sprint is the frame filled in for your business: memory about your work, rules for your risks, and jobs scheduled around your week.

Is my business data safe?

Claude's paid plans do not train on your data by default. In this setup the memory sits in your own files, and the rules stop anything being sent, deleted or paid without your yes. Treat those files like any private company document.
